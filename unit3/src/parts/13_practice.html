13Practice questions (PYQ pattern, one level harder)

These follow the shape of the end-sem Q3 questions but add one or two traps each (a weak entity owned by a weak entity, a ternary with an aggregate, a 1:1 that merges, a reverse-engineering question). Each answer was checked by the mapping program.

Practice P1design + minimum tables5 marks

A campus housing office records BUILDINGs (bid, bname). Each building has floors numbered 0, 1, 2 … (floor numbers repeat across buildings) with a wing name; each floor has rooms numbered 1, 2, 3 … within the floor, with a capacity. A PERSON (pid, name = first + last, several email ids) may occupy one room (recording since when); a room may hold several persons. Every person holds exactly one PASSPORT (passport_no, expiry) and every passport belongs to exactly one person. A person may mentor several other persons; each person has at most one mentor. Draw the ER diagram and convert it with the minimum number of tables.

Show answer
  • FLOOR is weak (owner BUILDING, partial key floor_no); ROOM is weak with owner FLOOR (a weak entity owning a weak entity), partial key room_no. ROOM’s key = (bid, floor_no, room_no).
  • PASSPORT–PERSON is 1:1 with total participation on both sides → merge into PERSON (passport_no becomes an alternate key).
  • OCCUPIES N:1 → FK (bid, floor_no, room_no) + since in PERSON (nullable — participation partial). MENTORS 1:N recursive → mentor_pid in PERSON.
  • email multivalued → separate table.

Practice P2ternary + aggregation5 marks

In an IPL auction database, a PLAYER signs a contract with a TEAM for a SEASON at a price. In one season a player plays for at most one team, but a team signs many players and a player can sign with different teams in different seasons. Each contract is negotiated by exactly one AGENT, who earns a commission on it; an agent negotiates many contracts. Draw the ER diagram (justify ternary vs binary and the need for aggregation) and convert it with minimum tables.

Show answer
  • CONTRACT involves player, team and season together → ternary. Labels: fixed (player, season) → at most 1 team ⇒ TEAM side “1”; fixed (team, season) → many players; fixed (player, team) → many seasons.
  • NEGOTIATES relates an AGENT to a contract — a relationship instance — so CONTRACT is aggregated. NEGOTIATES is N:1 from the aggregate to AGENT.
  • Key of CONTRACT = keys of non-“1” sides = (pid, year); team_id is a non-key FK.
{{erm:pr2}}

Because NEGOTIATES is N:1 (each contract one agent) it could also be folded into CONTRACT as agent_id + commission, giving 5 tables. Both are acceptable if you say which rule you used; the version above keeps CONTRACT free of NULLs for contracts signed without an agent.

Practice P3design + minimum tables5 marks

An online food-delivery app stores CUSTOMERs (cust_id, name, phone, several delivery addresses each made of house, street, city, pin), RESTAURANTs (rest_id, name, several cuisines, average rating computed from reviews), and menu items that are numbered within each restaurant (item_no, name, price). A customer places orders; each order is from exactly one restaurant, contains several menu items with quantities, has an order time, status and a total amount computed from its items, and is delivered by at most one DELIVERY_PARTNER (dp_id, name, vehicle_no). Draw the ER diagram and give the minimum relational schema.

Show answer
  • MENU_ITEM is weak (owner RESTAURANT). CONTAINS is M:N between FOOD_ORDER and MENU_ITEM — its table holds the whole key of MENU_ITEM (rest_id, item_no).
  • Derived: total_amount, avg_rating (not stored). Multivalued: cuisine; multivalued composite: address → one table with all components in the key.
  • PLACES, FROM_REST (total for orders) and DELIVERS (partial) are 1:N → FKs in FOOD_ORDER.

Practice P5reverse engineering4 marks

Draw the ER diagram from which the following tables were produced, stating the cardinality and participation of every relationship.

PUBLISHER(pub_name PK, city)
BOOK(isbn PK, title, pub_name NOT NULL REFERENCES PUBLISHER)
BOOK_author(isbn, author, PRIMARY KEY(isbn, author), FK isbn -> BOOK)
BOOK_COPY(isbn, copy_no, shelf, mid NULL REFERENCES MEMBER, issue_date,
          PRIMARY KEY(isbn, copy_no), FK isbn -> BOOK ON DELETE CASCADE)
MEMBER(mid PK, mname)
Show answer
  • BOOK_author has only (isbn, author) as key → author is a multivalued attribute of BOOK, not an entity.
  • BOOK.pub_name NOT NULL FK → PUBLISHES is 1:N with BOOK total.
  • BOOK_COPY’s key contains BOOK’s key plus copy_no and has ON DELETE CASCADE → weak entity owned by BOOK, partial key copy_no.
  • BOOK_COPY.mid is a nullable FK with issue_date beside it → BORROWS is 1:N (one member, many copies), copy participation partial; issue_date is an attribute of BORROWS.

Practice P6ternary keys3 marks

In a project-guidance relationship GUIDES(STUDENT, PROJECT, GUIDE): a student works on one project under a given guide, and on a given project a student has only one guide. Mark the cardinalities, give the candidate keys of the GUIDES table and explain why (roll_no) alone is not enough.

Show answer
  • Fixed (student, guide) → at most one project ⇒ PROJECT side “1”. Fixed (student, project) → at most one guide ⇒ GUIDE side “1”. Fixed (project, guide) → many students ⇒ STUDENT side N.
  • A “1” side is determined by the other two, so each “1” gives a candidate key made of the other two keys: {roll_no, fac_id} and {roll_no, proj_id}. One is the PK, the other is declared UNIQUE.
  • {roll_no} alone is not a key: a student can do two projects under two different guides.

Practice P7true / false with reason5 × 1 mark
  1. An entity type with total participation in a relationship must be a weak entity type.
  2. A 1:1 relationship never needs a separate table.
  3. An M:N relationship with total participation on both sides can be merged into one of the entity tables.
  4. For an overlapping, partial specialization, the “subclass tables only” mapping is correct.
  5. A relationship attribute of a 1:N relationship can be placed in the entity on the “1” side.
Show answer
  1. False. Weak means no key of its own. EMPLOYEE may be total in WORKS_FOR and still has emp_id.
  2. True in the standard mapping — the FK goes into one side (or the tables merge). (A separate table is allowed when both sides are partial and you want to avoid NULLs, but it is not needed.)
  3. False. Each entity can still be related to many of the other; one cell cannot hold several keys. M:N always needs its own table.
  4. False. Partial: superclass entities in no subclass would be lost. Overlapping: inherited data would be duplicated in several tables.
  5. False. Each “1”-side entity relates to many N-side entities, so it would need many values. Only the N side has one value.
Last-day checklist for an ER question
  1. Every entity has a key (or is weak with a partial key and double diamond).
  2. Every relationship has cardinality on both sides and the right participation lines.
  3. Special attributes are marked (composite, multivalued, derived) — the question usually names them.
  4. Assumptions are written in a box before the diagram.
  5. For “minimum tables”: merge 1:N and 1:1, keep M:N, n-ary and multivalued tables, merge 1:1 total-both; count and state the total.
