End-Sem 2024Q3(b)(i)2.5 marks

Justify the statement with the help of an example: “In ER to relational schema conversion, a multivalued attribute requires a separate relational table.”

Show answer

Take STUDENT(roll_no, name, gender) with a multivalued attribute mobile_no (a student may have several numbers).

{{er:r3}}

Why it cannot stay in the STUDENT table:

  1. Several values in one cell — (101, 'Asha', '98…, 97…') violates the relational rule that every value is atomic (1NF). Searching for one number or deleting one number becomes string manipulation.
  2. Fixed extra columns mobile1, mobile2, mobile3 — wastes space with NULLs for students with one number, and still fails for a student with four.
  3. One row per number — (101, Asha, F, 98…), (101, Asha, F, 97…): roll_no is no longer a key, and name/gender are repeated, causing update anomalies (change the name in one row and not the other).

Correct mapping: a separate table with the entity’s key and the attribute; the key of the new table is the combination:

{{map:r3}} 
CREATE TABLE STUDENT_mobile_no (
  roll_no   INT,
  mobile_no VARCHAR(15),
  PRIMARY KEY (roll_no, mobile_no),
  FOREIGN KEY (roll_no) REFERENCES STUDENT(roll_no) ON DELETE CASCADE);

Any number of phone numbers can be stored, nothing is repeated, and deleting a student deletes their numbers. Hence a multivalued attribute needs a separate table.

12.2 Weak entity types

End-Sem 2022Q2(a)4 marks

Explain weak entity set with example and how it is different from strong entity set.

Show answer

A weak entity set is an entity set that does not have a primary key of its own. Its entities can be identified only by being related to an entity of another (owner / identifying) entity set, combined with its own partial key (discriminator). It is existence-dependent on the owner and has total participation in the identifying relationship.

Example: LOAN(loan_no, amount) and PAYMENT(payment_no, pay_date, pay_amount). Payment numbers 1, 2, 3 … restart for every loan, so payment_no alone is not unique; (loan_no, payment_no) is.

{{er:t_weak}}

Notation: double rectangle (weak entity), double diamond (identifying relationship), dashed underline (partial key), double line (total participation). Table: PAYMENT(loan_no, payment_no, pay_date, pay_amount), loan_no FK → LOAN ON DELETE CASCADE.

End-Sem (year not printed)Q1(a)(iv)part of 4 marks

Highlight the key difference between strong and weak entities with the help of a suitable example.

Show answer

Key difference: a strong entity has a key of its own and exists independently; a weak entity has no key of its own — it is identified by its owner’s key plus its partial key and cannot exist without the owner.

Example: EMPLOYEE(emp_id, ename) is strong. DEPENDENT(dep_name, relation, dob) is weak: two employees can both have a dependent called “Riya”, so dep_name identifies a dependent only within one employee. Key = (emp_id, dep_name). Deleting an employee deletes their dependents.

End-Sem 2025Q3(b)(i)3 marks

We can convert any weak entity set to a strong entity set by simply adding appropriate attributes. Why, then, do we have weak entity sets? Can an identifying relationship of a weak entity type be of a degree greater than two? Give examples to illustrate your answer.

Show answer

Why weak entity sets

Yes — PAYMENT could be made strong by adding a unique payment_id, or by copying loan_no into it. We still use weak entity sets because:

  1. Avoids redundancy: if loan_no were also an attribute of PAYMENT, it would be stored both in the entity and in the LOAN_PAYMENT relationship; the two copies could disagree.
  2. Models reality: a payment “number 3” means something only for a particular loan; a dependent only for a particular employee. The weak entity expresses this existence dependency, so deleting the owner deletes its weak entities (cascade).
  3. No artificial keys: a surrogate id adds a meaningless attribute and an extra uniqueness rule to maintain.
  4. It tells the designer exactly how the key is formed during mapping (owner key + discriminator).

Degree greater than two — yes

A weak entity may depend on more than one owner. Then the identifying relationship is ternary (or higher) and the weak entity’s key = keys of all owners + its partial key.

Example: INTERVIEW is identified by the APPLICANT, the COMPANY and the interview date.

{{erm:t_weak3}}

Another example: a weak entity EXAM_SLOT identified by COURSE and SEMESTER together with slot_no.

12.3 Specialization, generalization, aggregation, roles

End-Sem 2025Q3(b)(ii)2 marks

Define the concept of aggregation. Give two examples of where this concept is useful.

Show answer

Aggregation is an abstraction through which a relationship set, together with its participating entity sets, is treated as a higher-level entity set, so that it can participate in another relationship. It is needed because the ER model cannot express relationships among relationships. It is drawn as a box around the relationship and its entities.

Example 1: EMPLOYEE works on PROJECT; a MANAGER monitors each (employee, project) assignment.

{{er:t_agg2}}

Example 2: a coaching CENTER offers a COURSE; a VISITOR enquires about a particular course at a particular centre (course notes p. 59). Other uses: a guarantor for a (customer, account) holding; an advisor who approves a (student, course) registration.

Without aggregation a ternary relationship would repeat every (employee, project) pair and could record monitoring of a pair that does not exist in WORKS_ON.

End-Sem 2024Q3(b)(ii)2.5 marks

Explain generalization, specialization and aggregation with the help of overlap and disjoint set examples.

Show answer

Specialization (top-down): defining subclasses of an entity type based on distinguishing characteristics; subclasses inherit all attributes of the superclass and add their own. Disjoint example: EMPLOYEE → SECRETARY, TECHNICIAN, ENGINEER — an employee belongs to at most one.

{{er:t_spec}}

Generalization (bottom-up): combining entity types with common attributes into a higher-level entity type. Overlapping example: EMPLOYEE and STUDENT generalized to PERSON — a teaching assistant is both.

{{er:t_overlap}}

Disjoint = an entity in at most one subclass; overlapping = an entity may be in several. (Completeness: total = every superclass entity in some subclass; partial = not necessarily.)

Aggregation: treating a relationship with its entities as an entity so it can take part in another relationship — e.g. MANAGER monitors (EMPLOYEE works_on PROJECT), diagram in the previous answer.

A Hospital Management System maintains information about doctors, nurses, patients, treatments and departments. Doctors and nurses are considered medical staff, while medical staff and administrative employees together constitute hospital staff. Every patient must undergo treatment prescribed by a doctor, whereas a department may or may not have doctors assigned to it.   • Specialization: MEDICAL_STAFF is specialized into DOCTOR and NURSE (“doctors and nurses are considered medical staff”) — top-down division by role. Disjoint and total (assumed).

Mid-Sem Sep 2026Q5(b)2 marks

Consider the statement: “Every employee has a manager, but a manager is also an employee.” Should Manager be modeled as a separate entity, an attribute of Employee, or a role played by Employee? Justify your answer.

Show answer

As a role played by EMPLOYEE, in a recursive relationship MANAGES (EMPLOYEE 1 —manager— MANAGES —subordinate— N EMPLOYEE).

  • Not a separate entity: a manager is an employee and has the same attributes (id, name, salary). A MANAGER entity would store the same person twice and the two copies could become inconsistent.
  • Not an attribute (manager_name): an attribute is only a value — it loses the link to the manager’s own employee record (salary, department) and breaks if two employees share a name.
  • Role: the same entity type participates twice in different roles; each employee has one manager, a manager has many subordinates. Mapping gives a self-referencing foreign key: EMPLOYEE(emp_id, name, …, manager_emp_id → EMPLOYEE).
{{erm:t_unary}}

(If managers had extra attributes, e.g. allowance, one would additionally specialize EMPLOYEE → MANAGER; the relationship still stays recursive.)

12.4 Convert a given ER diagram

End-Sem 2023Q3(c)4 marks

Explain the following concepts in an ER diagram: participation constraints and derived attributes. Convert the following ER diagram into the relational model with minimum number of relations.

{{er:p23}}
Show answer

Participation constraint — whether every entity of a set must take part in a relationship. Total (double line): every entity participates, e.g. every DEPARTMENT has a manager. Partial (single line): some may not, e.g. not every EMPLOYEE manages a department.

Derived attribute — an attribute whose value is computed from other attributes, e.g. age from dob; drawn as a dashed ellipse and not stored.

Conversion. An arrow from an entity to a relationship means each entity of that set takes part in at most one instance (key constraint):

  • A → R1: each A is related to at most one B ⇒ R1 is many-to-one; put b1 in A.
  • A → R2: each A related to at most one C ⇒ put c1 in A.
  • R3 has no arrows ⇒ many-to-many ⇒ separate table R3(b1, c1).
{{map:p23}}

Minimum number of relations = 4: A(a1, a2, b1, c1), B(b1, b2), C(c1, c2), R3(b1, c1).

This arrow style (entity → diamond) is Ramakrishnan’s key-constraint notation; your class notes use Korth’s style (diamond → entity). See section 4.4.

Many users can book the same ride for carpooling, and each user can also book many rides; therefore a Booking entity is required, which stores booking ID, status (confirmed/cancelled/completed) and payment mode. Additionally, every driver is assigned a parking spot, and one parking spot can belong to at most one driver at a time. Draw an ER diagram of the given problem statement.

  • APP_USER: composite name, multivalued phone, derived age. “Some users also act as drivers” → specialization APP_USER → DRIVER (partial).
  • DRIVER 1 — USES — N VEHICLE (a vehicle has one driver at a time).
  • APP_USER 1 — REQUESTS — N RIDE, RIDE total (“always requested by exactly one user”).
  • BOOKING entity resolves the M:N “users book rides”: APP_USER 1 — BOOKS — N BOOKING, RIDE 1 — FOR_RIDE — N BOOKING, BOOKING total in both.
  • DRIVER 1 — ALLOTTED — 1 PARKING_SPOT, DRIVER total (“every driver is assigned a spot”).
