9SQL vs NoSQL for an application

NoSQL is in the Unit-2 syllabus (full notes: Unit 2 §11), but end-sem Q1(c) of 2024, 2025 and 2026 and mid-sem 2024 Q5(b) ask it as a “which database for this application” question. This page is the short version you need for those answers.

NoSQL

NoSQL (“Not only SQL”) databases store data in models other than tables, usually with a flexible schema, built to scale horizontally across many cheap servers and to handle huge volumes of semi-structured or unstructured data at high speed. Most trade strict consistency for availability (BASE: Basically Available, Soft state, Eventually consistent) instead of ACID.

Decision rule for scenario questions

Money, stock counts, anything that must never be wrong or half-done → RDBMS (ACID). Huge volume, fast-changing structure, read-heavy feeds, user-generated content, logs, relationships-as-graph → the matching NoSQL type. Large real systems use both (polyglot persistence).
