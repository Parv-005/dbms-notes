0How to use these notes

  • Syllabus covered: Structure of relational databases, integrity constraints (primary key, foreign key, unique, not null, check), enforcing integrity constraints, relational algebra and calculus, introduction to SQL (DDL, DML, DCL), aggregate functions, sub-queries, correlated sub-queries, GROUP BY, HAVING, ORDER BY, joins and their types, triggers, introduction to NoSQL, SQL vs NoSQL.
  • Every result table is real. All relational-algebra results were computed by a small RA engine, and all SQL outputs were produced by actually running the query (SQLite) on the sample tables shown. If a table says “Output: 4 rows”, those are the 4 rows you would get.
  • Relational algebra and calculus get the most space (sections 3–4) because they are new and because the PYQs ask them almost every year (Sailors–Boats–Reserves, Students–Courses, Suppliers–Parts, bank database…). Section 3.14 is a pattern cookbook: once you know its 13 patterns you can write any exam query.
  • Boxes: Definition exact wording to reproduce, Example, Tip, Common mistake, Exam what is asked and how, Beyond syllabus useful extras (marked, safe to skip on a first read).
  • Sections 14–15 contain every Unit-2 PYQ with full exam-style answers, then harder practice questions (PYQ pattern, one level up). Section 12 is a revision sheet and section 13 has MCQs. Use the ▾ Answers button in the sidebar to open/close all answers.
  • Corrections: a few statements in the source notes were wrong or ambiguous; they are flagged in grey “Correction” boxes where they occur.
