0How to use these notes

  • Syllabus: Normalization: functional dependencies, axioms, closure, normal forms based on primary keys, 1NF, 2NF, 3NF, BCNF, lossless and dependency-preserving decomposition, converting a relation into a required normal form.
  • How Unit 4 is examined. End-sem Q4 (Q3 in the 2022 and 2023 papers): three 5-mark parts, attempt two. Almost every part is one of these five types:
    1. “Find the candidate key(s)” / “number of superkeys”.
    2. “Find the highest normal form”.
    3. “Decompose into 2NF / 3NF / BCNF”.
    4. “Is this decomposition lossless? Dependency preserving?”
    5. Theory: anomalies, Armstrong’s axioms, closure, minimal cover, why NULLs and spurious tuples are bad.
    Each type has a fixed method in these notes (section 9 collects them on one page).
  • Every answer is computed, not just written. A small program (closure, candidate keys, normal-form test, chase test, dependency-preservation algorithm, 3NF synthesis, BCNF decomposition) re-checks every key, normal form and decomposition in these notes each time the page is built. The closure lines, test tables, chase tableaux and preservation tables you see are generated by that program, so they cannot contain copying slips.
  • Your class notes (FD types, 1NF colour table, 2NF/3NF/BCNF examples, lossless and dependency-preservation algorithms) and Dhruv’s notes are used throughout; where an example in them has a slip, it is pointed out in a Common mistake box.
  • Notation: single capital letters are attributes, AB means the set {A, B}, X+ is the closure of X. Boxes: Definition Example Tip Common mistake Exam Beyond syllabus.
